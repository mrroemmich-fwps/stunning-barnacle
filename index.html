    Words to know

    Investing vocabulary

    Principal

    The money you start with or put in.

    You put $1,000 in. Your principal is $1,000.

    Interest

    Money the bank pays you for keeping your money there. (Or money you pay when you borrow.)

    2% interest on $1,000 = $20 a year.

    Rate

    The percent of interest or growth per year.

    A savings account rate of 2%.

    Compound interest

    Earning interest on your interest. Your money grows faster the longer you wait.

    Year 1: $1,000 → $1,070. Year 2: you earn on $1,070, not $1,000.

    Saving

    Putting money aside somewhere safe for later.

    Savings account, GIC.

    Investing

    Putting money into something you hope will grow. It might also shrink.

    Funds, stocks, crypto.

    Return

    How much your money grew or shrank, shown as a percent.

    $100 became $110. Return = +10%.

    Risk

    The chance you could lose money. Higher risk can mean bigger wins and bigger losses.

    MoonCoin is high risk. A GIC is low risk.

    Volatility

    How much a price bounces up and down. Big bounces = high volatility.

    A bumpy line on the chart.

    Stock (share)

    A tiny piece of one company. If the company does well, your piece is worth more.

    1 share of MapleByte Tech.

    Index fund

    One purchase that holds pieces of hundreds of companies at once.

    Canada index fund = 200+ companies.

    Diversification

    Spreading money around so one bad thing can't sink you. "Don't put all your eggs in one basket."

    4 different chips instead of 10 on one stock.

    Missions

    • Gr 8 + Gr 9

      Under the mattress

      Take every chip off. Play 100 futures. Did you ever lose money? Read the note about prices rising. Is cash really "safe"?

    • Gr 8

      Steady vs bouncy

      Put all 10 chips on Savings. Play 100 futures and add it to your table. Now try all 10 on the Canada index fund.

      Which had the higher typical ending? Which one ever lost money?

    • Gr 8 + Gr 9

      All eggs, one basket

      Put all 10 chips on MapleByte Tech. Count the red squares. Then spread chips over Grocers, Prairie Power, MapleByte and the World index fund.

      How did spreading out change the red squares?

    • Gr 8 + Gr 9

      Time is your friend

      Use all 10 chips on the Canada index fund. Test 2 years, then 25 years.

      In which time frame is losing money more likely? Why?

    • Gr 9

      The MoonCoin mystery

      MoonCoin has the best "average year" on its card. So why do so many futures end in red?

      Hint: if you lose 50%, how much do you need to gain to get back to where you started?

    • Gr 9

      Build for a goal

      Build one portfolio for college in 4 years and another for retirement in 40 years. Test both.

      Explain your choices using risk, volatility and time.

    Teacher notes
    Target outcomes
    • CEFL8 FL 8.5 saving vs investing, return, risk, principal, rate, compound interest (the core of this lab)
    • CEFL9 FL 9.5 (understanding) compound interest amplifies growth and debt; time and rate are the key variables. Bridge to the Unit 4 credit card payoff: the same curve that grows savings grows a balance.
    • MATH8 N11 / MATH9 N12 percents · MATH8 SP2 / MATH9 SP1 interpret graphs · MATH9 SP5 trends, extremes, clusters · MATH8 SP7 same experiment, different outcomes · MATH9 SP7 probability vocabulary (more/less likely, best/worst)
    • ELA9 3.2.2 fact vs opinion (extension: compare a real "get rich fast" crypto ad with what the 100-futures test shows)
    Side-by-side pathways
    • Grade 8: Missions 1–4. Record the comparison table on paper. Sentence frame: "In ___ out of 100 futures I lost money. The safest choice was ___ because ___."
    • Grade 9: Missions 3–6. Write a short recommendation for a younger student, using risk, volatility, diversification and time horizon. Preview of Unit 4: debt grows with the same math.
    Differentiation
    • Plain-language cards and a readable typeface; read the vocabulary cards aloud together first.
    • The 100-square grid lets students count outcomes instead of calculating percents (100 squares = 100%).
    • Pair a strong reader with a weaker reader on missions; one runs the simulator, one records.
    • Extension: ask why the "average year" on a card is not the same as what usually happens (volatility drag).
    About the numbers All companies and coins are made up. Growth rates and bounce sizes are rough, illustrative versions of long-run patterns (cash ≈ 0%, savings ≈ 2%, bonds ≈ 4%, broad stock funds ≈ 7% with large swings). The simulator does not recommend any real product. Each "future" number uses the same market luck when students change chips, so they see the effect of their choices; "Play another future" changes the luck.
    Made-up companies and numbers for learning only. Real investments behave differently, and past results never promise future ones.
